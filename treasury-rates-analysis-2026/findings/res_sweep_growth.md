# growth

## SUMMARY
US growth data from late June through August 27-28, 2026 tell a sharply two-track story. The labor market deteriorated abruptly: June nonfarm payrolls printed +57K on July 2 (vs ~115K expected), with April cut to +148K and May to +129K. Then the July report (released Aug 7) showed payrolls FALLING 23K — vs +83K consensus — with May revised down again to +63K and June slashed to just +20K (two-month revision -103K). Losses were concentrated in government (-53K, incl. local education -50K) and retail (-19K); private payrolls rose only +30K. Yet the unemployment rate FELL to 4.1% (from 4.2%) because labor force participation slid to 61.4%, a 5-year low — a shrinking-supply story (immigration/participation), not a layoff story. Confirming that, initial jobless claims sat near 60-year lows: 189K in mid-July, 209K (w/e Aug 1), 207K (w/e Aug 15), 203K (w/e Aug 22), with continuing claims at 1.778M. ADP July private payrolls (+44K, Aug 5) echoed the hiring slowdown. Wage growth cooled to 3.2% y/y, lowest since May 2021. August payrolls/ADP are not yet released (due Sept 2-4).

Activity data, in contrast, look strong. Q2 GDP grew 1.5% annualized (advance, July 30; unrevised in the Aug 26 second estimate) after 2.1% in Q1 — modest, but with consumer spending revised UP to 3.4% annualized. ISM manufacturing jumped from 53.3 in June to 55.6 in July — the highest since May 2022, with production at 58.5 and employment (52.8) expanding for the first time in 33 months; ISM services held at 54.1 (though its employment index fell back to 47.4). The August S&P Global flash composite PMI surged to 56.0, the fastest growth since April 2022. Retail sales rose 0.2% in June but fell 0.6% in July (still +5.0% y/y). Most striking: Atlanta Fed GDPNow for Q3 opened near 5.0%, spiked to 6.2% (Aug 3) and 5.8% (Aug 6), then settled to 4.3% (Aug 14), 4.03% (Aug 18) and 4.6% (Aug 26) — a boom-like nowcast.

Equities are at/near record highs. The S&P 500 set its 27th record close of 2026 during August, closing at a record 7,798.99 and trading above 7,800 intraday (7,814.88) around Aug 12-13 after a cool CPI; it ended the window at 7,711.76 (Aug 28), ~1% off the record after a winning week. The Dow crossed 54,000 in early August and closed at 53,559.99 (Aug 28). The Nasdaq's record close of 27,086.81 dates to June 1; at 26,402.42 (Aug 28) it sits ~2.5% below that peak after a summer slump and Nvidia-led chip wobbles.

Bottom line on the key question: the economy is NOT uniformly softening — it is bifurcated. Payrolls alone (negative July print, huge downward revisions) would normally argue for LOWER yields. But output/demand indicators (GDPNow ~4.6%, ISM 55.6, flash PMI 56.0, 3.4% consumer spending, record equities) signal reaccelerating growth with a supply-constrained labor market and sticky ~3.4% CPI. That mix gives fundamental cover for higher real yields via growth/inflation; a long-end rise is only "anomalous" if one weights the deteriorating payroll data — where falling participation, not layoffs, drives the story. Risk assets clearly trade the strong-growth interpretation.
