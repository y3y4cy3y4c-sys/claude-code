# term-premium

## SUMMARY
The summer 2026 long-end selloff looks like a term-premium/fiscal repricing, not a monetary-policy repricing. The NY Fed ACM 10-year term premium averaged 0.513% in June 2026 (down from 0.667% in May), then jumped to 0.87% for July and stood at 0.80% on August 13, 2026 (latest daily print surfaced; FRED's THREEFYTP10 was updated through Aug 14). That is roughly a 30-35bp rise from late June to mid/late August, and it accounts for most of the ~25-30bp rise in the 10-year yield (weekly-average ~4.44% at end-June vs 4.68% on Aug 27) plus the bigger 30-year move (5.31% close on Aug 17, highest since 2007; intraday above 5.33% on Aug 18, a 19-year high). The San Francisco Fed's alternative Christensen-Rudebusch model put the 10-year term premium at 1.33% on Aug 10, 2026 vs 1.20% a year earlier — model estimates span roughly 0.80%-1.35%. Crucially, the expected-rates component moved the OTHER way: after Jackson Hole (Aug 21, where incoming/new chair Kevin Warsh spoke), September cut odds rose from ~72% to ~94%, with about two cuts priced — yet long yields rose. Ferrante Capital's decomposition states it cleanly: "the expected short-rate path has moved down (two cuts now priced), term premium has moved up (supply-demand shift), net effect close to zero... the bond market does believe the Fed... the cuts priced in are being offset by investors finally demanding compensation for duration risk." CNBC (Aug 18) reports strategists ascribe the run that began in June to a deficit set to eclipse 2025's (July's monthly deficit was the largest since March 2021), inflation stuck above 2%, and a rash of corporate issuance — US companies have sold nearly $1.7 trillion of bonds YTD, +27% y/y. Barclays sees the rise "less about inflation and more about the U.S. budget deficit, high levels of issuance related to AI competing with Treasurys, and higher term premiums"; Barclays' Anshul Pradhan: "What is notable today is not the existence of these pressures, but that they appear strong enough to overwhelm individual soft-data releases." Bloomberg (Barbuscia/Xie/MacKenzie, Aug 17) notes AI-linked long-dated corporate supply (+$474bn net) has more than offset the $440bn drop in net Treasury coupon supply. BMO flagged global fiscal concerns (US, Japan, UK, Europe); Fundstrat's Mark Newton sees long yields pushing to 5.60-5.70%. Comparisons: the episode echoes October 2023 (term premium +~150bp in Q3 2023 as the 10-year ran to 4.99% on deficit fears) and January 2025 (ACM above 0.8%, then the highest since 2011) more than April 2025's tariff shock. Caveat: the late-August daily ACM print (Aug 20-28) was not directly retrievable via search; the Aug 13 daily (0.80%) and July monthly (0.87%) are the freshest confirmed ACM readings, and the June-to-July monthly jump (0.513%→0.87%) is the cleanest measured term-premium move in the window.
