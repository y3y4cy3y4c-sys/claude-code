# The Steepener Defense — Interview Q&A (one page)

**5s30s DV01-neutral steepener · as of Aug 27–28, 2026**
Entry **+74–80bp** (Fri 8/28 close ≈ +74 · H.15 8/27: +81) · Initial target **+110–120** · Stretch **+150** · Stop **+45–50** · DV01 **$20k/bp** per leg · Carry **modest +** · Risk at stop **0.6% NAV** · 1y Z **−1.3** / percentile **4%** (flattest of past year)

> Framing note: main answers deliberately use the relative-value formulation — the research memo's stronger language ("phantom hiking cycle", "fiscal dominance") is toned down here by design. Numbers below the line are follow-up ammunition, not the lead.

## The 30-second answer — say this first

"My trade is a DV01-neutral 5s30s Treasury steepener: long the 5-year, short the 30-year. The relative-value thesis is that the 5-year is more sensitive to the medium-term Fed and growth path, while the 30-year increasingly reflects fiscal supply, term premium and structural demand for capital. If growth weakens, the market should eventually price a less hawkish Fed and the 5-year should outperform. If growth stays resilient but fiscal issuance and capital demand stay strong, the long end can remain under pressure.

So I don't need an outright call on whether yields rise or fall — I need the 30-year to sell off more, or rally less, than the 5-year. The main risk is credible renewed tightening: if the Fed actually delivers hikes and successfully compresses long-run inflation and term premia, the curve bear-flattens and I lose."

---

**Q1 — What exactly do you make money on?**
P&L ≈ DV01 × (Δy30 − Δy5); I win whenever the 30y moves up more, or down less, than the 5y. The 5-year does not need to rally and the 30-year does not need to sell off: −40/−10 → +30 win; +20/+50 → +30 win; +60/+20 and −20/−60 → −40 lose. A relative-value macro expression, not a directional duration bet.
*If pushed:* DV01-neutral at $20k/bp per leg = +$44.9mm 5y vs −$13.2mm 30y face; a parallel move nets to ~zero to first order.

**Q2 — So is your thesis "the Fed is wrong"?**
No. The front-end repricing after Jackson Hole is a genuine response to sticky inflation. My claim is about *relative* pricing: "the market may be overpricing the persistence of Fed hawkishness, while underpricing the persistence of structural pressure at the long end." The mispricing is in the 5y versus the 30y, not in any single point.
*If pushed:* priced ~35% Sept hike, ~1 hike by end-26; eroding it: payrolls −23k, participation falling, AHE +3.2% — below inflation, no wage-price spiral to break.

**Q3 — What's the asymmetry, in one breath?**
"Either weaker growth eventually removes the hike pricing, helping the 5-year — or persistent inflation and fiscal pressure remain concentrated at the long end, hurting the 30-year. I don't need to claim current market prices are logically inconsistent." Both resolutions steepen; only credible delivered tightening flattens.
*Careful:* don't argue hike odds contradict 2.28% breakevens — the market can price a hike *because* it expects it to keep medium-term inflation anchored. The trade doesn't need that claim.

**Q4 — If it's a Fed trade, why not 2s30s?**
"I considered 2s30s, but that makes the trade too dependent on the next few FOMC meetings. The 2-year is dominated by near-term meetings, whereas the 5-year has more exposure to how the entire medium-term policy path reprices over my six-to-twelve-month horizon."
*If pushed:* 2s30s captured ~1.6× of 5s30s's steepening in deep cycles but needs ~2× the balance sheet ($118.6mm vs $58.2mm gross), and its +29bp static carry is mostly the hike premium itself — realized only if the Fed doesn't hike.

**Q5 — Why not 10s30s?**
"Both legs of 10s30s are heavily exposed to long-duration term premium, so the policy-path view gets diluted. Five years is the balance: it still responds strongly to a medium-term Fed repricing, while thirty years is where I want the fiscal and term-premium exposure."
*If pushed:* 10s30s kept only ~0.33× of the deep-cycle steepening — the relative-value version, not the macro expression.

**Q6 — Why short the 30y at 5.19%, the highest yields since 2007?**
"I'm not short the 30-year because I think 5.2% is fundamentally cheap. I'm short it because, relative to the 5-year, its ability to rally is constrained by fiscal supply, term premium and structural demand for capital." The 30y is where duration supply, real term premium (2.96%), absorption capacity and capital demand are most visible — even a productive AI-investment boom raises capital demand and long real rates.
*If pushed:* 20s30s is flat (5.18/5.19) — term premium fully priced by the 20y point; August's buyback-driven rallies retraced within days. The short leg only needs the 30y to be unable to rally far.

**Q7 — What's the carry?**
"The trade has modest positive carry and roll, so I'm not paying heavily to wait for the thesis." Funds sit below the whole coupon curve, so the front leg earns over repo; the flat 20s30s makes the short leg's roll roughly zero.
*If pushed:* ≈ +10bp/yr in curve terms by aging both legs and repricing on an unchanged curve (running +$125k, roll +$71k at this size) — assumption-sensitive in magnitude, robust in sign.

**Q8 — How is it sized, and where are you wrong?**
Risk-budget sizing: 0.6% of NAV at a −30bp stop → $20k/bp curve DV01 (daily VaR ≈ 0.11% NAV). The stop at +45–50 is where the thesis is falsified — the market believing in delivered tightening. Exit on information before price: a Board governor voting to hike; two payroll prints above +150k or wages back above 3.5%; the 30y sustainably through ~4.90 without a panic.
*Note:* scenario probabilities are subjective priors — no Kelly, no point-estimate EV.

**Q9 — What does history say about steepeners in easing cycles?**
"Meaningful easing cycles have tended to steepen 5s30s substantially — but shallow insurance-cut cycles followed by renewed tightening are the key failure mode, and 1998 is the obvious example." That failure mode is exactly what the stop and falsifiers are built around.
*If pushed:* 8 cycles since 1984: deep cycles peaked +180–230bp from the first cut (median ~18m in); 1998 flattened −50bp in 12m. Initial target +110–120 = regaining 2025's steepness (traded +101 in Jun-25) — needs cuts to resume, not a recession.

**Q10 — The curve already steepened 100bp since 2024 — aren't you late?**
"On a three-year window the spread is only mid-range — Z +0.5, 57th percentile — because that window mixes the flat 2023–24 regime with the steep 2025 one. But on a one-year window it sits at the flattest level of the past year: Z −1.3, bottom five percent. The 2026 hike-repricing retraced about a quarter of the 2024–25 steepening, and that retracement is what I'm buying — a re-entry into an established regime, not a chase."
*If pushed:* 5s30s +7 (Apr-24) → +110 (late-25) → +81 (8/27 H.15) → ≈+74 Friday close (1y Z ≈ −1.9 at that level); 1y mean 96.4, σ 12.0. Same picture in 2s30s and 10s30s — the whole curve flattened on hike bets. Stats: `spread_stats_5s30s.py`.

**Q11 — How do you implement it, and what's the event path?**
Cash 5y/30y, or futures: long FV against short Ultra Bond (its ≥25y deliverable basket tracks the 30y; the classic contract's 15–25y basket doesn't), "sized dynamically to equalize DV01 off the current cheapest-to-deliver"; swap version: receive 5y / pay 30y SOFR. Path: Sep 4 payrolls, Sep 10 CPI, Sep 15–16 FOMC — watch the dissent composition more than the decision — then Q4 refunding and the December FOMC. Hike-premium repricing is a 1–4-month story; the cut-pricing and fiscal legs run 6–12 months, funded by the carry.

**Q12 — What kills the trade?**
Credible renewed tightening: hikes delivered, believed, and long-run inflation and term premia compress — a bear-flattener, capped at −30bp by the stop. The smaller tail is a haven bull-flattening into the 30y. Both are the same admission: the structural pressure at the long end turned out to be cyclical.
